# Balance Issues — Lux's RPG

Outstanding balance problems only. Anything already fixed has been removed from this document —
see [BALANCING_CHANGES.md](BALANCING_CHANGES.md) for what shipped in 4.2.0 and why.

Audited against v4.1.0; every finding below was re-checked against the current working tree.
All damage and cost figures are computed from the formulas as written, with no items purchased
and `dmgmult` at its base `100n`.

**15 findings open** — 3 critical, 8 major, 4 minor.

Closed since the last pass: the whole mana section (regen, max-mana scaling and Efficiency Mastery
cost), the Physical tree's missing level scaling, and two property-name typos that were breaking
Lux's pacifist dialogue and Bob's visit counter.

**Ruled intentional, not listed as issues:** healing-item overheal; Sloth's forced-1 damage and
absurd HP pool (it's representing Sloth); Glass Cannon attack values, since they have bullet
patterns of their own and are therefore dodgeable; Gambler's Coin having a 15% chance of being
positive as it relates to how gambling is in real life; and the Frying Pan's absurd damage.

## 1. Spells

### 1.1 — CRITICAL — Poison Spray outdamages everything by three orders of magnitude
`spells.js:824–841`, `combat.js:829–836`

Applies `400 × lv²` stacks. The tick consumes half and deals `stacks × lv`, so the first tick alone is `200 × lv³`.

| Level | Poison Spray tick 1 | Nature's Fury (same tree) | Comet (Astral cap) | Kitsune max HP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 5,400,000 | 4,200 | 60,000 | 24,000,000 |
| 50 | 25,000,000 | 7,000 | 100,000 | 40,000,000 |
| 100 | 200,000,000 | 14,000 | 200,000 | 80,000,000 |

> **Effect:** a 7-SP Druid spell is ~3,500× the tree's own damage capstone and kills every enemy including world bosses in one or two ticks — and although poison immunity now works, the damage is so far ahead of every HP pool that resisting it barely matters. Druid is not a healing class in practice; it is the strongest damage class by a margin nothing else approaches.

### 1.2 — MAJOR — Three signature capstones are strict downgrades
`spells.js:435` (Eldritch Blast), `spells.js:665` (Thunderbolt), `spells.js:1173` (Iceshock)

Damage at level 50, before multipliers:

| Tree | Capstone | SP | DMG | Predecessor | SP | DMG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Astral Mage | Eldritch Blast | 18 | 21,000 | Prism Blast | 16 | 153,000 |
| Stormmancer | Thunderbolt | 12 | 1,820 | Ion Cannon | 10 | 19,380 |
| Cryomancer | Iceshock | 10 | 1,560 | Frost Prison | 8 | 19,380 |

The intent is legible — trade raw damage for low mana cost plus a status rider. The trade no longer even works on its own terms: since 4.2.0 mana genuinely costs something, yet all three capstones are worse than their predecessor on damage **per MP** as well as in absolute terms — Thunderbolt 40 dmg/MP vs Ion Cannon 78, Iceshock 52 vs Frost Prison 97, Eldritch Blast 10 vs Prism Blast 96.

> **Effect:** the most expensive purchase in three trees is a button never pressed again after buying it. Worse, these are the exact skills gating Lux / Azmodan / Kitsune spawns, so players are pushed to buy them and then discover they are downgrades.

### 1.3 — MAJOR — Neutral spells roll their damage three times per cast
`combat.js:175`, `combat.js:648–711`, `spells.js:1506–1737`

The `dmg` getters call `Math.random()` on every read. The tooltip reads `s.dmg` to display a number; `cast()` reads it again to apply damage; then the `sid === 'campfire' / 'cauterize' / 'cosmicBlessing'` blocks roll a third time and apply a *second* independent effect.

> **Effect:** the displayed damage is never the damage dealt. Cosmic Blessing resolves twice per cast — potentially two separate damage instances, or damage plus a full heal. Any player hovering a Neutral spell to compare options reads a number generated by a die roll unrelated to the cast.

### 1.4 — MINOR — Stormmancer backfire carries no risk
`combat.js:463–473`

Backfire deals `dmg / 4`, is clamped with `BigMath.max(p.hp, 1n)` so it can never kill, and does not prevent the spell from firing in full.

> **Effect:** Ion Cannon's advertised 20% backfire chance is an occasional HP tax against an exponentially growing HP pool. The red underline and tooltip warning promise a downside the class does not have.

## 2. Enemies

### 2.1 — CRITICAL — Greed one-shots any player who has ever earned money
`enemies/sins.js:69–97`, `combat.js:1140–1147`

`atk` is `p.totalGold / 2n` — lifetime gold earned, which never decreases. Spawn gate is `totalGold >= 1000n`. Each hit also steals `random(0..499) × p.totalGold` gold.

> **Effect:** unwinnable for every player reaching level 50, since total gold is then in the millions and player HP in the hundreds of thousands. The gold theft is always larger than the balance, so it reads as "lose everything" every time.

### 2.2 — MAJOR — Envy and Wrath scale off unbounded counters
`enemies/sins.js:132–160`, `enemies/sins.js:37–65`

- Envy: `skills.length × 500 × lv`. `p.skills` includes weapon-granted actions, so equipping Nox Nocturnal adds three entries and `1500 × lv` to Envy's attack.
- Wrath: `kills × lv`, no ceiling.

> **Effect:** both punish the exact behaviour the game rewards everywhere else. A completionist at level 60 with 40 skills faces 1.2M damage per hit from Envy; a player at 10,000 kills faces 600,000 from Wrath. Neither is survivable and neither is signposted before the fight starts.

### 2.3 — MAJOR — Attack values mean two different things
`combat.js:946`, `combat.js:1129–1156`, `combat.js:1176`

An enemy with a bullet pattern runs for `5000–10000ms` with 500ms i-frames — up to ~20 applications of `enemy.atk` per turn. An enemy without one applies it exactly once. Every enemy stat block is written as though it hits once.

> **Effect:** effective enemy damage varies 20× based on a property the stat block doesn't mention, and the variance within that is player dodging skill rather than character power. Pattern length is randomised per turn, so the same enemy can deal 4× or 20× its listed attack on consecutive turns.

### 2.4 — MAJOR — The Player's Mirror spawns on day one and doubles your gold
`enemies/world_bosses.js:46–48`, `enemies/world_bosses.js:96–99`, `world_bosses.js:121`

`canSpawn: () => p.skills.length >= 2`. Starting skills are `['strike', 'heal']`, so the gate is satisfied before the player acts. Its gold reward is `p.gold` — the player's current balance.

> **Effect:** a weight-2.5 world boss in the level-1 encounter pool, and the only enemy whose payout scales with the player's bank. Killing it at 10M gold pays 10M gold. The comment says "at least one spell beyond strike", which needs `>= 3`.

### 2.5 — MINOR — Kitsune pays negative EXP and gold
`enemies/lux_and_co.js:80–87`, `combat.js:1356–1359`

Both getters return `-(10000n × scaledLV)`. `win()` adds them directly, so killing Kitsune subtracts gold and zeroes the EXP bar.

> **Effect:** plausibly intentional as a Lux-flavoured punishment, but nothing warns the player, the victory log still reads "Looted", and `formatNumber` renders the negative reward as a loot line. If deliberate, it needs different copy.

### 2.6 — MINOR — Will O' Wisp is a different string in three places
`enemies/bosses.js:99`, `bulletPatterns/bosses.js`, `sub-Combat1.js:35`

Enemy is `"Will O' Wisp"`; its bullet pattern and gem-chance key are `"Will o' Wisp"`. It is the only non-Gerald enemy with no pattern match.

> **Effect:** falls back to a single plain attack instead of a 5–10s pattern, making it dramatically easier than the three bosses beside it. Gem chance silently drops from 25% to the 10% default.

## 3. Items and the economy

### 3.1 — CRITICAL — Treasure Map returns a million times its cost
`shop.js:222–255`, `shop.js:188–221`

Ancient Treasure Map pays `200 × 1.75^(lv-1)` and costs `40 × 1.15^(lv-1)`. Different growth rates, so the ratio compounds.

| Level | Map cost | Map payout | Return | Ancient Dmg Mult. cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 141 | 30,800 | 218× | 19,200 |
| 30 | 2,300 | 2.2×10⁹ | 970,000× | 6.4×10⁷ |
| 50 | 37,700 | 1.6×10¹⁴ | 4.3×10⁹× | 2.1×10¹¹ |

> **Effect:** one map purchase funds every remaining shop item forever. **This is load-bearing** — without it the shop is unbuyable, because enemy gold is linear (`1200 + 1200 × scaledLV` for a Vampire King) while prices multiply by 1.1–1.5 per level. Fixing the map without flattening shop price curves makes the whole shop inaccessible past level 30.

### 3.2 — MAJOR — Healing item rarity is inverted and the tiers are meaningless
`items.js:100–153`, `items.js:81–99`

At level 50 (max HP ≈ 758,000):

| Item | Rarity | Heal | % of max HP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood Stone | Rare | 5,000 | 0.66% |
| Medium Blood Stone | Epic | 7,500 | 0.99% |
| Large Blood Stone | Legendary | 10,000 | 1.3% |
| Abbie's Apple | Epic | 500,000 | 66% |
| Health Vial | Common | full | 100% |

> **Effect:** the three-tier Blood Stone progression spans 0.66%–1.3% of a health bar — a Legendary worth less than the Common Health Vial at every level above 2, while an Epic apple out-heals it 50×. Any linear `K × lv` heal is dead on arrival against a `1.2^lv` health pool. This is about the *magnitudes*: the tiers do not separate from each other or track their rarities.

### 3.3 — MAJOR — Damage multiplier sources differ by two orders of magnitude
`items.js:1310–1450`, `shop.js:352–441`

Shop multipliers grant flat `+5` to `+25` at exponentially rising prices. Tarot cards grant level-scaled percentages: The Tower `+20 × lv`, The Emperor / The Chariot / Strength `+5 × lv` each.

> **Effect:** at level 50 one Tower card equals 40 Ancient Damage Multipliers, and the Ancient costs 2.1×10¹¹ gold. The entire shop damage line is obsolete the moment the player opens one Rare loot box. Tarot cards are infinitely repeatable, so `dmgmult` has no ceiling from three separate directions (shop, cards, War Mastery).

### 3.4 — MAJOR — Loot box level grants skip the stat growth
`lootboxes.js:145`, `lootboxes.js:157`

`p.lv += 1n` and `p.lv += p.lv * 3n` write the level directly rather than going through `addExperience`, where the ×1.2 growth on `mhp` / `mmp` / `msn` lives.

> **Effect:** the jackpot roll is a net nerf. Enemy stats scale off `p.lv`, so quadrupling the level quadruples incoming damage while leaving the health pool untouched. The most exciting result in the 5,000-gem box makes the run harder.

### 3.5 — MINOR — Two "Ancient" upgrades are strictly worse buys
`shop.js:104–123`, `shop.js:144–163`

Eldritch Orb grants `30 + mmp × 0.5`; Ancient Eldritch Orb grants `60 + mmp × 0.5` at double the base price. Pure Insight / Ancient Pure Insight have the same relationship. Only the flat term differs, and it stops mattering around level 5.

> **Effect:** the Uncommon costs twice the Common for an identical multiplier. Ancient Dragon Heart got it right (75% vs 50%), so match that pattern.

## 4. Sanity is hard to lose

Sanity has exactly two effects in the codebase:

1. At `sn <= 0`, the daily rest drains `50 × day` mana instead of restoring it (`maincode.js` → `handleDailyResources`).
2. The in-combat regen tick is gated behind `p.sn > 0n` (`combat.js` → the passive regen block).

Both penalties are paid in mana. Until 4.2.0 mana was effectively free, which made them meaningless;
now that regen is a fixed share of your maximum and costs scale with the pool, **losing your Sanity
genuinely hurts.** That much is fixed.

What remains is the other half: `msn` grows ×1.2 per level while every sanity drain in the game is
linear in level. At LV 50 an un-itemised player has ~758,000 max Sanity, and the heaviest drain on
the roster (Will O' Wisp, `80 + 80 × scaledLV`) removes 2,720 a hit — about 278 hits to empty.

> **Effect:** the penalty is real but almost unreachable after the early game, so three shop items,
> a full spell branch (Mind Shield, Dual Heal, Shielding Dark) and the five Sanity Drainers still
> have very little to push against. Same shape as the level-curve problem: linear pressure against
> a compounding pool.

## 5. Where to start

Ordered by change-in-play per line of code.

- **Rein in Poison Spray.** `400 × lv²` stacks means a first tick of `200 × lv³` — 25 million at LV 50, roughly 3,500× the Druid tree's own damage capstone, and now that poison immunity works it is the single strongest thing in the game. A 7-SP spell should not be. (§1.1.)
- **Pick one growth curve.** Player stats compound (mhp/mmp/msn ×1.2 per level, plus 1%-of-current per mastery point) while every enemy is linear in `p.lv`. Difficulty now gives players a per-save lever on this, but Easy keeps the original curve by design. Either give enemies a `1.2^lv` term or flatten level-up growth to additive — most of section 3 resolves itself once they match.
- **Give Greed and Envy an exit.** A flee action, a damage cap, or spawn gates that read player power rather than player level. (§2.1, §2.2.)
- **Re-express healing as a percentage** rather than `K × lv`, so the rarity tiers actually separate. (§3.2.)
- **Decide what the Treasure Map is for.** Its payout curve outruns its own price by a factor that compounds, so one purchase funds the rest of the game — but it is load-bearing, because enemy gold is linear while shop prices are exponential. Flatten the shop curves first or the shop becomes unbuyable. (§3.1.)
- **Fix the three capstones**, which are now worse than their predecessors on damage per MP as well as outright, and are the exact skills gating the world-boss spawns. (§1.2.)
